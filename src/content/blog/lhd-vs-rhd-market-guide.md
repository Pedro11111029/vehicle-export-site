Steering side is one of the first questions a vehicle exporter should ask, and one of the most expensive to get wrong. A right-hand drive (RHD) vehicle sent to a left-hand drive (LHD) market is difficult to register, hard to resell and may need a conversion that costs more than the vehicle is worth. This guide explains how to decide correctly.

## The basics

- **LHD (left-hand drive)** — the steering wheel is on the left. Traffic keeps to the right.
- **RHD (right-hand drive)** — the steering wheel is on the right. Traffic keeps to the left.

Roughly two-thirds of the world drives on the right and uses LHD vehicles. The remaining third — including several large vehicle-importing markets — drives on the left.

## Country quick reference

| Region | Traffic side | Typical configuration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Europe (most), USA, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, most of Africa | Right | LHD |
| China, Russia, Central Asia, Middle East (most) | Right | LHD |
| UK, Ireland, Cyprus, Malta | Left | RHD |
| India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka | Left | RHD |
| Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Nigeria | Left | RHD |
| Japan, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Hong Kong | Left | RHD |
| Caribbean (Jamaica, Trinidad, Barbados, Guyana) | Left | RHD |

Some markets are genuinely mixed:

- **Cyprus and Malta** are RHD but import both configurations.
- **Ethiopia** recently moved toward RHD-only registration.
- **Guyana and Suriname** differ from their neighbours.
- **Middle East** is mostly LHD, but some ports re-export RHD stock to East Africa.
- **Island markets** often accept both because supply is thin.

Always confirm the current rule with your customs broker or registration authority. Rules change, and an outdated assumption is a costly one.

## Why the configuration matters beyond driving

### Registration and homologation

Many countries require a certificate of conformity or a type approval that is tied to the steering side. Even where registration is technically possible, insurers may decline coverage or charge a premium for a vehicle configured for the wrong side of the road.

### Resale value

A wrong-side vehicle is a niche product. Buyers for it are few, and they know it. In most markets an LHD car in an RHD country (or the reverse) sells at a discount of 15–30% — if it sells at all.

### Parts and service

Steering racks, dashboards, pedals, wiring harnesses and headlight patterns differ between LHD and RHD. A dealer with no RHD parts supply will struggle to service the fleet, which matters a great deal to taxi and fleet buyers.

### Headlights

Headlight beam patterns are direction-specific. RHD headlights dip to the left, LHD headlights dip to the right. Misaimed headlights fail many roadworthiness inspections.

## How orders usually mix

For a typical container programme we see:

- **Pure LHD markets** — 100% LHD, sometimes with a preference for European-style or US-style specifications.
- **Pure RHD markets** — 100% RHD. Factory RHD allocation is tighter than LHD, so lead times are often longer.
- **Mixed markets** — usually 80/20 or 90/10, with the minority configuration ordered only against confirmed customer orders.

If you are unsure, order a single sample unit first. It is far cheaper to test the market with one vehicle than to discover after a container arrives that half the stock cannot be registered.

## Ordering checklist

Before placing an order, confirm:

1. The traffic side and registration rule in the destination country.
2. Whether the importer needs a type approval or certificate of conformity.
3. The headlight beam pattern required.
4. Whether the dashboard language and units (km/h vs mph) suit the market.
5. The availability of parts and service for that configuration locally.
6. The resale market for the configuration if the buyer does not complete the sale.
